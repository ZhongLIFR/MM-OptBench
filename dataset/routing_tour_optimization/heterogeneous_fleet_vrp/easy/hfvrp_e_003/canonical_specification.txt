Canonical specification for a deterministic single-depot Heterogeneous Fleet Vehicle Routing Problem instance.

Interpretation:
- Each activated vehicle starts at the depot, serves a subset of customers, and returns to the depot.
- Each customer must be visited exactly once by exactly one activated vehicle.
- Travel between every pair of distinct nodes is allowed, so the routing graph is complete.
- Vehicle capacities, fixed activation costs, and per-distance cost multipliers depend on the chosen vehicle.
- If a vehicle is activated, its fixed activation cost is incurred exactly once.
- Variable travel cost on arc (i,j) for vehicle k equals alpha_k times the base distance delta[i,j].
- There are no time windows, multiple depots, split deliveries, stochastic demands, or customer-vehicle incompatibilities.
- The optimization goal is to minimize total fixed-plus-variable routing cost.

Travel-cost definition:
- For every vehicle k and ordered pair of distinct nodes (i,j), travel_cost[i,j,k] = distance_multiplier[k] * base_distance[i,j].

Decision variables:
- x[i,j,k] in {0,1}: equals 1 if vehicle k directly travels from node i to node j
- y[k] in {0,1}: equals 1 if vehicle k is activated
- u[i,k] >= 0: vehicle-specific MTZ/load helper variable for customer i and vehicle k

Objective:
- Minimize total fixed activation cost plus total variable travel cost across all activated vehicles.

Constraints:
- Each customer has exactly one outgoing service arc across the fleet.
- Each customer has exactly one incoming service arc across the fleet.
- For each vehicle, flow is conserved at every customer it visits.
- Depot departure and depot return are each equal to the vehicle-activation variable.
- The total demand assigned to a vehicle cannot exceed that vehicle's capacity.
- Vehicle-specific MTZ-style constraints eliminate subtours and enforce capacity-consistent route order.
- Arc and activation variables are binary; load/helper variables are continuous.
