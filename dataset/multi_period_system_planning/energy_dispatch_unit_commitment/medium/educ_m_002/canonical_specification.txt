Canonical specification for a deterministic single-zone medium-level energy dispatch and unit commitment instance.

Interpretation:
- For each generator and period, a binary commitment decision determines whether the generator is off or on.
- If a generator is off in a period, its output must be zero in that period.
- If a generator is on in a period, its output must lie between its minimum and maximum output limits.
- In every period, total generation across all generators must exactly equal the system demand of that period.
- A startup cost is incurred whenever a generator changes from off in the previous period to on in the current period.
- A shutdown cost is incurred whenever a generator changes from on in the previous period to off in the current period.
- Ramp-up and ramp-down limits apply between consecutive periods.
- Minimum up-time and minimum down-time requirements apply to each generator.
- The initial commitment status before the first period is given explicitly for every generator.
- No transmission, reserve, stochastic, or multi-zone constraints are present.

Decision variables:
- p[g,t] >= 0: generation output of generator g in period t
- u[g,t] in {0,1}: equals 1 if generator g is on in period t
- v[g,t] in {0,1}: equals 1 if generator g starts up in period t
- w[g,t] in {0,1}: equals 1 if generator g shuts down in period t

Objective:
- Minimize the total marginal generation cost plus total startup and shutdown costs across all generators and periods.

Constraints:
- Demand balance for every period: sum_g p[g,t] = D[t].
- Minimum output for every generator-period: p[g,t] >= Pmin[g] * u[g,t].
- Maximum output for every generator-period: p[g,t] <= Pmax[g] * u[g,t].
- Commitment transition definition: u[g,t] - u[g,t-1] = v[g,t] - w[g,t], using the stated initial status for the first period.
- Ramp-up constraints between consecutive periods.
- Ramp-down constraints between consecutive periods.
- Minimum up-time constraints based on startup decisions and commitment persistence.
- Minimum down-time constraints based on shutdown decisions and off-status persistence.
- Nonnegativity of generation and binary domains for commitment, startup, and shutdown variables.
