Canonical specification for a deterministic single-zone energy dispatch and unit commitment instance.

Interpretation:
- For each generator and period, a commitment decision determines whether the generator is off or on.
- If a generator is off in a period, its output must be zero in that period.
- If a generator is on in a period, its output must lie between its minimum and maximum output limits.
- In every period, total generation across all generators must exactly equal the system demand of that period.
- A startup cost is incurred whenever a generator changes from off in the previous period to on in the current period.
- The initial commitment status before the first period is given explicitly below for every generator.
- No ramping, minimum up-time, minimum down-time, reserve, transmission, load shedding, or stochastic constraints are present.

Sets:
- Generators G = ['G1', 'G2', 'G3', 'G4']
- Periods T = ['t1', 't2', 't3', 't4']

System demand by period:
- t1=44, t2=57, t3=49, t4=53

Generator parameters:
- G1: marginal_cost=8, startup_cost=120, min_output=20, max_output=34, initial_status=0
- G2: marginal_cost=10, startup_cost=90, min_output=10, max_output=24, initial_status=0
- G3: marginal_cost=14, startup_cost=46, min_output=0, max_output=18, initial_status=0
- G4: marginal_cost=19, startup_cost=18, min_output=0, max_output=16, initial_status=0

Decision variables:
- p[g,t] >= 0: generation output of generator g in period t
- u[g,t] in {0,1}: equals 1 if generator g is on in period t
- v[g,t] in {0,1}: equals 1 if generator g starts up in period t

Objective:
- Minimize the total marginal generation cost plus total startup cost across all generators and periods.

Constraints:
- Demand balance for every period: sum_g p[g,t] = D[t].
- Minimum output for every generator-period: p[g,t] >= Pmin[g] * u[g,t].
- Maximum output for every generator-period: p[g,t] <= Pmax[g] * u[g,t].
- Startup definition for every generator-period: v[g,t] >= u[g,t] - u[g,t-1], with u[g,t0] equal to the given initial status.
- Nonnegativity of generation and binary commitment/startup variables.
