Canonical specification for a deterministic single-zone hard-level energy dispatch and unit commitment instance.

Interpretation:
- The system operator chooses generator commitment and dispatch decisions across a multi-period planning horizon.
- If a generator is off in a period, its output must be zero in that period.
- If a generator is on in a period, its output must lie between its minimum and maximum output limits.
- In every period, total generation across all generators must exactly equal system demand.
- A startup cost is incurred whenever a generator changes from off in the previous period to on in the current period.
- Generator output changes are restricted by ramp-up and ramp-down limits between consecutive periods.
- Minimum up-time and minimum down-time constraints apply to each generator.
- The initial commitment status and initial output before the first period are specified explicitly below.
- No transmission network, reserve, stochastic, load-shedding, or multi-zone constraints are included.

Decision variables:
- p[g,t] >= 0: generation output of generator g in period t
- u[g,t] in {0,1}: equals 1 if generator g is on in period t
- v[g,t] in {0,1}: equals 1 if generator g starts up in period t

Objective:
- Minimize the total marginal generation cost plus total startup cost across all generators and periods.

Constraints:
- Demand balance for every period: sum_g p[g,t] = D[t].
- Minimum output for every generator-period: p[g,t] >= Pmin[g] * u[g,t].
- Maximum output for every generator-period: p[g,t] <= Pmax[g] * u[g,t].
- Startup definition for every generator-period: v[g,t] >= u[g,t] - u[g,t-1], with u[g,t0] equal to the given initial status.
- Ramp-up and ramp-down constraints between consecutive periods, with the first period linked to the given initial output.
- Minimum up-time constraints requiring commitment persistence after startup.
- Minimum down-time constraints requiring off-status persistence after shutdown.
- Nonnegativity of generation and binary commitment/startup variables.
